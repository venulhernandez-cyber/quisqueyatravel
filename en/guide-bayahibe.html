🤿 Bayahibe

Bayahibe: the Fishing Village That's the Gateway to Saona and Catalina

Everyone who visits Saona Island passes through Bayahibe without staying. That's the mistake. Bayahibe is a fishing village on the southeast coast with two beaches of its own, some of the best dive spots in the whole Caribbean, and the shortest crossing — genuinely the shortest — to Saona and Catalina. Here's why it's worth staying, not just passing through.

Why Bayahibe, Not Just "a Stop" on the Way to Saona

Most tourists only know Bayahibe as the port where they board the boat to Saona Island. That's selling it short. The village itself has its own beach, seafront seafood restaurants, colorful fishing boats, and an atmosphere much more authentic than the Punta Cana or Bávaro hotel zone. And if you stay right there, you skip the 1 to 1.5-hour bus ride that travelers from Punta Cana have to endure to reach Saona — the boat leaves practically from your hotel.

The closest airport is La Romana International Airport (LRM), only 20-25 minutes away. If you can't find a direct flight, Punta Cana (PUJ) is 1-1.5 hours away and usually has more options from US cities.

The Two Beaches: Bayahibe and Dominicus

Bayahibe Beach is the village beach — public, relaxed, with fishing boats bobbing along the shore and barefoot restaurants where you eat fresh fish for $10-15. It's small but full of real character, without the "resort in a straight line" feel of other areas.

Dominicus Beach, a 5-10 minute walk or golf-cart ride away, is the other side of the coin: wide white sand, shallow turquoise water, and the row of all-inclusive resorts (Dreams Dominicus, Viva Wyndham, Iberostar, Be Live, among others). This is where you go if you want the classic Caribbean postcard with all the comfort of an all-inclusive.

The smart move: stay at a resort in Dominicus for the comfort, and head down to Bayahibe village in the afternoons for fresh fish and sunset with the fishermen. The best of both worlds, 10 minutes apart.

World-Class Diving (Seriously)

If you dive or want to learn, Bayahibe is probably the best spot in all of eastern DR. It sits within and next to Cotubanamá National Park, with protected coral reefs, underwater walls, and visibility that on good days exceeds 20 meters. The classic dive is the St. George shipwreck, a 1980 cargo ship sunk on purpose as an artificial reef, now covered in coral and full of fish.

For something different, there are the freshwater cenotes like Padre Nuestro — inland caves and pools of crystal-clear water, popular both for cave diving and for a calmer snorkel if you're not a certified diver. Local operators right in the village offer everything from discovery dives to full certifications.

Diving tip: book with a local Bayahibe dive shop instead of through the resort — they're usually cheaper and know the spots better depending on the day's conditions.

The Shortest Crossing to Saona and Catalina

Bayahibe is the official departure port for Saona Island and also for Catalina Island, the smaller, less crowded alternative off La Romana. From here, the boat crossing to Saona takes between 20 and 45 minutes — compared to the 9-10 hours door-to-door that someone leaving from Punta Cana suffers just for the bus transfer. If Saona is on your list, staying in Bayahibe gives you back half a vacation day.

What to Know Before You Go

Bayahibe is a small village — don't expect Punta Cana-style nightlife or shopping malls. It's quiet, ocean- and dive-focused, ideal for couples, divers, and families who prefer something more intimate than a giant resort complex. If you're looking to party, this isn't your destination; if you want to truly disconnect with good food and clear water, it's one of the best spots on the island.

Rainy season runs from May to November, with short afternoon showers. For diving, the best visibility is usually between December and April, when the sea is calmer.

Frequently Asked Questions About Bayahibe

Bayahibe or Punta Cana for diving?

Bayahibe, hands down. It has the best dive spots in eastern DR: protected coral reefs inside Cotubanamá National Park, the St. George shipwreck, and freshwater cenotes like Padre Nuestro. Punta Cana has a wider beach, but for diving Bayahibe wins by a mile.

Bayahibe Beach or Dominicus Beach?

Bayahibe Beach is the village beach: public, with fishing boats, more authentic and relaxed. Dominicus Beach, 5-10 minutes away, is where the all-inclusive resorts are: wider sand, classic postcard turquoise water, but busier. Many visitors do both in the same day.

How much does a trip to Bayahibe cost?

A 4-night trip for two people at an all-inclusive resort in Dominicus runs between $1,000 and $1,500 total, flights included. At a boutique hotel in the village with meals outside, it can drop to $700-1,000.

How do you get from Bayahibe to Saona Island?

Bayahibe is the official departure port to Saona Island — the catamaran or speedboat crossing takes between 20 and 45 minutes, far less than the 1-1.5 hour bus ride tourists departing from Punta Cana or Bávaro need.

Which airport is best for Bayahibe?

La Romana International Airport (LRM) is only 20-25 minutes away and the fastest option. Punta Cana Airport (PUJ), 1-1.5 hours away, usually has more direct flights from the United States and sometimes lower fares.
