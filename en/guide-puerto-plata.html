⛰️ Puerto Plata

Puerto Plata: the North Coast Almost No One in the Diaspora Knows

In Puerto Plata you can ride the cable car, swim through the 27 Waterfalls of Damajagua, relax on Playa Dorada, and kitesurf in Cabarete — all for less than Punta Cana costs. There's a conversation among Dominicans that almost never happens: the one about Puerto Plata. Everyone knows Punta Cana. A lot of people have been to the capital. But the north of the island — that green stretch between the mountains and the Atlantic — is still a secret to the diaspora. And that's exactly why it's worth going now.

Why the North Instead of Punta Cana

Puerto Plata has something Punta Cana can't give you: real variety. It's not just resort and beach — it's an authentic town, colonial history, mountains, adventure, and three coastal towns each with a completely different vibe. And price-wise, the north consistently comes in cheaper on flights, hotels, and activities.

Flying to Puerto Plata means landing at Gregorio Luperón Airport (POP), 20 minutes from the hotel zone. No 3-hour drive from SDQ. That's time back for the family.

The La Loma Cable Car — the Experience That Sets This Trip Apart

If you go to Puerto Plata and don't ride the cable car, it's like going to Paris and skipping the Eiffel Tower. The La Loma Isabel de Torres cable car climbs 2,600 feet above sea level. At the top there are botanical gardens, a Christ statue (similar to Rio's), and a view of the entire north coast that leaves you speechless. Kids go crazy for it. It costs around $10 USD per person and it's worth every cent.

The cable car opens in the morning and closes before 5pm — plan to go early, especially in July when tourism picks up.

Cable car tip: Go on a weekday morning — on summer weekends the line can run over an hour. Bring cash in pesos, since the ticket booth doesn't always take cards.

The Three Beaches That Define the North Coast

Playa Dorada is the all-inclusive resort zone. Golden sand, calm water, and the comfort of never having to go anywhere. If you want pure relaxation — eat, swim, lie by the boardwalk — this is your spot. It has several well-regarded resorts at lower prices than Punta Cana.

Sosúa, 15 minutes away, is different: a calm bay with water so clear you can see the bottom 20 feet down. The snorkeling here is some of the best on the island. There's a main strip with seafood restaurants where you can eat well for $8–15. More local, less resort.

Cabarete is the north's nightlife and adrenaline hub: the world capital of kitesurfing, beach bars, music late into the night, and an international crowd that mixes Europeans, Dominicans, and travelers of every kind. Not for young kids at night, but the beach is spectacular by day. You can learn kitesurfing with certified instructors — a 3-hour lesson starts at $80 USD.

The 27 Waterfalls of Damajagua — Non-Negotiable

30 minutes south of Puerto Plata are the 27 Waterfalls of Damajagua. Natural freshwater pools connected by drops and rock slides you climb and jump through. You go in groups with a guide and a helmet. You can do the first 7, 12, or all 27 — the 7-waterfall tour is perfect for families with kids 8 and up.

  • 7-waterfall tour — 2-3 hours, easy-medium level, great for families
  • 12-waterfall tour — 3-4 hours, medium level, more jumps
  • 27-waterfall tour — full day, adventure level

The official park price is around $10–15 USD per person. Book ahead in high season — July fills up.

What Else to See While You're There

San Felipe Fortress on the Puerto Plata boardwalk is pure history: built in the 16th century to protect the city from pirates, today it's a museum and lookout. The Amber Museum downtown explains where Dominican amber comes from — the best in the world, by the way — and has pieces with 25-million-year-old insects trapped inside. Both cost under $5 USD.

If you want to get away from the coast, the road toward San José de las Matas or the Bajabonico River offers authentic Dominican countryside scenery. The north is the DR of the past — quiet, real, and free of crowds.

What You Should Know Before You Go

Most of Puerto Plata's rain falls between May and November — but that doesn't mean it rains all day. It's usually short afternoon showers. Morning sun almost always shows up. Bring something waterproof if you're going to the 27 Waterfalls.

POP airport gets direct flights from several US cities. Check JetBlue and American first — they usually have good fares on this route. Book at least 3 weeks ahead in July.

Puerto Plata isn't Punta Cana, and that's its biggest strength. You're going to see the real DR — with everything that means. If you go with curiosity and a willingness to explore, the north of the island will surprise you. And when you get home, you'll be the one who knows what everyone else hasn't discovered yet.

Frequently Asked Questions About Puerto Plata

How much does the Puerto Plata cable car cost?

The La Loma Isabel de Torres cable car costs around $10 USD per person. It climbs 2,600 feet and includes botanical gardens and a full view of the north coast. It opens in the morning and closes before 5pm.

What's the best beach in Puerto Plata?

It depends what you're after: Playa Dorada for all-inclusive resorts and pure relaxation, Sosúa for snorkeling in clear water and a local vibe, and Cabarete for kitesurfing and nightlife. All three are 15-20 minutes apart.

How much does a trip to Puerto Plata cost?

A 4-night trip for two people at an all-inclusive hotel in Playa Dorada runs $1,100–1,600 total, flight included. At a boutique in Sosúa with food outside, it can run similar or less.

What are the 27 Waterfalls of Damajagua?

Natural freshwater pools connected by rock slides and small waterfalls, 30 minutes from Puerto Plata. You visit in a group with a guide and helmet; the 7-waterfall tour (2-3 hours) is great for families, and costs $10-15 USD per person.

When is the best time to visit Puerto Plata?

Most rain falls between May and November, but it's usually short afternoon showers with morning sun. July gets busier, so book at least 3 weeks ahead.
