🏖️ Beach Ranking

The Best Beaches in the Dominican Republic, According to Someone Born There

Everyone has an opinion on "the best beach in the DR" — but most people have only seen Punta Cana. I was born on the island and have spent months covering destination after destination for this site, so here's the honest ranking: from the untouched sand of Bahía de las Águilas to the resort comfort of Playa Bávaro. None of these beaches is "the best" in any absolute sense — it depends on whether you want to disconnect from the world or never leave the hotel pool. I'll tell you which is which.

1Bahía de las Águilas, Barahona — the Untouched Caribbean Beach

If you want sand without a single resort umbrella in sight, this is the beach. Five miles of fine sand, no hotels, no noise — just turquoise water, sun, and you. You get there by a dirt road and then a short boat ride from Pedernales, and that's exactly why almost nobody goes. Everyone who's been says it's the prettiest beach in the Caribbean. I believe them.

How to get there: drive to Pedernales or Las Salinas and take a short boat ride from there. Arrange the boat in advance during high season. Read the full Barahona guide →

2Playa Rincón, Samaná — Top-10 Caribbean Beach with No Hotel in Sight

Playa Rincón has been named one of the best beaches in the Caribbean more than once, and you understand why the moment you see it: almost 2 miles of sand, palm trees, and coconuts, with not a single hotel built in front of it. You get there by boat from Las Galeras or by car on a dirt road (4x4 recommended in rainy season). If Bahía de las Águilas is too far for you, Playa Rincón is your second-best untouched beach option.

How to get there: boat from Las Galeras or car on a dirt road. Combine it with Las Terrenas if you're traveling with family. Read the full Samaná guide →

3Isla Saona and Punta Cana — the Easiest to Plan

Playa Bávaro has over 18 miles of soft, white sand and the calmest water on the island — the most comfortable option if you're traveling with young kids. And if you want to get out of the resort zone without complicating things, the Isla Saona excursion is the one I recommend most: you go out by catamaran, stop at a natural pool in the middle of the sea, and arrive at a beach that looks like it's out of a movie. A full-day trip runs $60-80 per person.

How to get there: catamaran or boat tour from Punta Cana or Bayahibe, book ahead. Read the full Isla Saona guide → · Punta Cana Guide →

4Sosúa, Puerto Plata — the Clearest Water for Snorkeling

Sosúa is a calm bay with water so clear you can see the bottom 20 feet down — the best snorkeling on the island, without exaggeration. It has a main strip with seafood restaurants where you can eat well for $8-15, and the vibe is more local, less resort than Punta Cana or downtown Puerto Plata. Playa Dorada is 15 minutes away if you'd rather have all-inclusive comfort.

How to get there: 15 minutes by car from downtown Puerto Plata or POP airport. Read the full Puerto Plata guide →

5Isla Catalina, La Romana — the Calmer Alternative to Saona

Isla Catalina is the classic beach escape from La Romana — white sand, coral reef close to shore, great for snorkeling. It's calmer and less crowded than Punta Cana's Isla Saona, so if you've already been to Saona or want fewer crowds, this is your pick. La Romana also has Altos de Chavón, the Mediterranean-style village built over the Chavón River, if you want to pair beach time with something different.

How to get there: boat or catamaran tour from the Casa de Campo marina or Bayahibe. Read the full La Romana guide →

6Playa Dorada, Puerto Plata — All-Inclusive Without Punta Cana's Price Tag

Playa Dorada is the north coast's all-inclusive resort zone: golden sand, calm water, and the comfort of never having to go anywhere. If you want pure relaxation — eat, swim, lie by the boardwalk — this is your spot, and it costs less than an equivalent resort in Punta Cana. A 4-night trip for two people can run $1,100–1,600 total, flight included.

How to get there: minutes from Puerto Plata's international airport (POP). Read the full Puerto Plata guide →

7The Malecón, Santo Domingo — Not for Swimming, but Not to Be Missed

The Santo Domingo Malecón isn't the beach where you go swimming — but it's one of the prettiest coastal promenades in the Caribbean, and no one in the Dominican diaspora should skip it. Walk it at sunset after touring the Zona Colonial. It doesn't compete with the sand beaches on this list, but it completes the picture: the DR isn't just resort, it's also a living capital city with the sea as its backdrop.

How to get there: within Santo Domingo, right next to the Zona Colonial. Read the full Santo Domingo guide →

The Truth Nobody Tells You

There's no such thing as "the best beach in the DR" — there's the best beach for what you want out of that particular trip. If you're traveling with young kids and want zero complications, Bávaro or Playa Dorada. If you want to truly disconnect from the world, Bahía de las Águilas or Playa Rincón. If snorkeling is your thing, Sosúa or Isla Catalina. The Dominican Republic has a beach for every kind of traveler — the mistake is thinking Punta Cana is the only option.

Frequently Asked Questions About DR Beaches

What is the best beach in the Dominican Republic?

It depends what you're looking for. For untouched sand with no hotels, Bahía de las Águilas in Barahona and Playa Rincón in Samaná are among the best in the Caribbean. For resort comfort, Playa Bávaro in Punta Cana and Playa Dorada in Puerto Plata are the easiest to enjoy.

Isla Saona or Isla Catalina?

Isla Saona (from Punta Cana or Bayahibe) has more tour options and the famous natural pool. Isla Catalina (from La Romana) is calmer, less crowded, and better for reef snorkeling. If you want fewer crowds, Catalina wins.

Can you visit Bahía de las Águilas in one day?

Yes, but it takes planning: you drive to Pedernales or Las Salinas and then take a short boat ride. From Santo Domingo it's about 4-5 hours each way, so it's best to spend a night in Barahona.

Which beach is best for families with kids?

Playa Bávaro in Punta Cana and Playa Dorada in Puerto Plata — calm water, low current, and resort infrastructure with everything close by. Sosúa also works well for kids who already swim, thanks to the clear water and low waves.

Which is the least touristy beach in the DR?

Bahía de las Águilas, by far — 5 miles of sand with not a single hotel in front of it. Playa Rincón in Samaná is the second most untouched option, though it already gets more visitors in high season.
