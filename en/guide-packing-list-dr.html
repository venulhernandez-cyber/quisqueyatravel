What to Pack for the Dominican Republic

Every time someone in the family is about to take their first trip to the DR, I get the same messages: "do I need an adapter?", "do I need repellent?", "how much clothing do I pack?". Here's the honest list — the one put together by someone who was born there, not a generic blog that's never set foot on the island. What you actually need, and what you can leave home because it's cheaper there.

Clothing: Less Than You Think

It's hot in the DR twelve months a year, so pack by activity type, not by "day count." For a week, 4 to 5 beach outfits (swimsuits, shorts, light t-shirts) is more than enough — you can do laundry at the hotel or buy something if you run short, clothes are cheap there.

  • Light, quick-dry clothing — thick cotton feels heavy in the humidity
  • At least one modest outfit (long pants or a skirt, a shirt with sleeves) for church, government errands, or formal restaurants
  • A light sweater or jacket if you're heading to mountain areas like Jarabacoa or Constanza — it actually gets cold up there
  • Sandals for the beach and comfortable closed-toe shoes for walking
  • A hat or cap — the midday sun in the DR shows no mercy

Documents: What You Can't Forget

A valid passport (check it doesn't expire within 6 months of your entry date), your flight confirmation, your hotel address, and the digital entry form the DR has required since 2023. If you're not sure exactly what paperwork you need for your passport, I break it all down in the 2026 travel requirements guide — don't leave it for the airport.

Tip: take a photo or save a PDF of your passport and cards on your phone (and send it to someone you trust, too). If something gets lost, having it handy saves you hours of headache.

Money and Tips: What Nobody Explains

Bring some cash in dollars or Dominican pesos (RD$) for tips, corner stores, and beach vendors — not everyone accepts cards outside the hotel zone. A 10% tip is standard at restaurants (though many already include it on the bill as "servicio"), and a few loose pesos for whoever watches your car or helps with your bags go a long way. Exchange some money on arrival or withdraw from an airport ATM — the street exchange rate is almost always better than the hotel's exchange counter.

Health: Mosquitoes, Sun, and Water

The Caribbean sun is stronger than you think, even when it's cloudy — bring sunscreen, ideally one that won't harm the reefs (no oxybenzone or octinoxate) if you're going diving or snorkeling. For mosquitoes, a repellent with 25-30% DEET is the most effective, especially at dawn and dusk and in rural or mountain areas. Don't drink tap water — ask for bottled water, which costs pennies at any corner store.

  • Sunscreen (ideally reef-safe)
  • Mosquito repellent with DEET
  • Basic first-aid kit: pain relievers, something for an upset stomach, band-aids
  • Personal medications in their original container, with a prescription if possible

Electronics: the Detail Almost Nobody Knows

If you're coming from the United States or Canada, good news: the DR uses the same voltage (110V) and the same plug type (A and B) you do, so you don't need an adapter. If you're coming from Europe, the UK, Australia, or any country with different plugs, you do need one. A 10,000-20,000 mAh power bank is essential for full beach or excursion days without a place to charge. And consider a data eSIM if you don't want to rely on hotel wifi — link below.

What You Can Leave Home

You don't need to overstuff your bag with beach towels (almost every hotel provides them), a hair dryer (resorts have one), or a charger for every single device — one multi-tip cable saves space. And don't worry about bringing extra snacks or basics: at any supermarket or corner store in the DR you can get almost everything you need, often cheaper than in the United States.

With this list you won't overpack or land on the island with stuff you'll never use. Pack light, leave room for what you'll bring back — because you always buy something — and enjoy the trip.

Frequently Asked Questions About What to Pack for the DR

Do I need a power adapter to travel to the DR?

If you're coming from the United States or Canada, no. The Dominican Republic uses the same plug type (A and B) and the same voltage (110V). If you're coming from Europe, the UK, or any country with type C, F, or G plugs, yes, you need an adapter.

Can you drink tap water in the DR?

It's not recommended. Most hotels and resorts provide free bottled or filtered water. On the street, buy sealed water bottles — they cost very little and are sold at any corner store (colmado).

What should I wear to church or a government office in the DR?

Pack at least one modest outfit — long pants or a skirt, a shirt with sleeves — if you plan to visit a church, handle an official errand, or dine at a formal restaurant. Beachwear stays at the beach.

Is mosquito repellent necessary in the DR?

Yes, especially if you leave the hotel zone or go to rural or mountain areas. Bring repellent with 25-30% DEET, and use it especially at dawn and dusk, when mosquitoes are most active.

How much clothing should I pack for a week-long trip to the DR?

Don't pack an outfit for every single day — the DR is hot year-round and you can wash or buy something if you run short. For 7 days, 4-5 beach outfits, 2 evening-out outfits, and one modest change of clothes is more than enough.
