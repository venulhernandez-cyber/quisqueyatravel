🏔️ Jarabacoa

Jarabacoa: the Mountain Guide Nobody Sends You

In Jarabacoa you can swim at Salto de Jimenoa, go rafting on the Yaque del Norte river, and stay in cool mountain cabins — the perfect escape when Punta Cana is at its most expensive and crowded. There's a conversation a lot of us in the Dominican diaspora never had: the one about Jarabacoa. We ask family about Punta Cana, about the capital, about Samaná — and Jarabacoa gets left out. That's a mistake. This town in the mountains of the Cordillera Central is one of the island's best-kept secrets, and it's perfect for summer — when Punta Cana is at peak price and peak heat.

Why Jarabacoa in the Summer

While the rest of the DR sits around 95°F in July, Jarabacoa stays cool at about 72-79°F. They call it "the City of Eternal Spring" and they're not exaggerating. If you're coming from a hot climate, arriving in Jarabacoa feels like being able to breathe again. Kids have a blast because they can stay active without melting. And the scenery — rivers, green mountains, waterfalls — barely feels like the same country as the Santo Domingo Malecón.

Jarabacoa is about 2.5 hours from Santo Domingo via the Duarte Highway. The logistics: international flight to SDQ + rental car or transfer from the airport.

The Waterfalls You Have to See

The starting point is the waterfalls. Salto de Jimenoa 1 drops 115 feet through rocks and jungle — to get there you cross an old hanging bridge that's a little dizzying but adds to the thrill. Salto Baiguate is more accessible, great for families with kids. With a half-day excursion you can see both.

  • Salto de Jimenoa 1 — 115-ft drop, jungle trail
  • Salto Baiguate — wider, ideal for families
  • Salto de Jimenoa 2 — the most spectacular, requires more hiking
Best time for the waterfalls: Go early in the morning (before 10am) — the water is clearer, there are fewer groups, and the light for photos is better. Summer weekends fill up fast with local excursions.

What to Do Beyond the Waterfalls

Rancho Baiguate is the spot for the adrenaline crowd: rafting on the Yaque del Norte river, canyoning, ziplines, and camping. For something calmer, the trails around town and the strawberry farms are a different kind of experience — yes, the DR grows strawberries, and in Jarabacoa they sell them fresh by the roadside.

Parque La Confluencia is where the Yaque del Norte and Jimenoa rivers meet — locals go there to swim. And if you have a car, the climb up Pico Duarte starts from a nearby town — it's a 3-day hike to the highest peak in the Caribbean.

Where to Eat

Jarabacoa's cooking leans more country than coast: mountain sancocho, chicharrón with tostones, fried trout raised in fresh river water. Downtown has several colmados and small restaurants. Rancho Camp David on the outskirts has good food and a mountain view that's worth the trip on its own.

What You Should Know Before You Go

Jarabacoa isn't a resort destination. No beach, no all-inclusive — if that's what you're after, check our guide to the best beaches in the Dominican Republic. This is a mountain town with real people and logistics that require some planning. You need a car — there's no public transportation between the attractions. The road to some waterfalls can get muddy during rainy season (May–September). Bring cash.

But if you do it right — with a car, a good cabin, and two or three days — Jarabacoa shows you a version of the DR most tourists never see, and one that a lot of the Dominican diaspora has never experienced either. That, brother, is worth the trip.

Frequently Asked Questions About Jarabacoa

Why visit Jarabacoa in the summer?

While the rest of the DR sits around 95°F in July, Jarabacoa stays cool at about 72-79°F — hence the nickname City of Eternal Spring. It's the perfect escape when Punta Cana is at its most expensive and crowded.

Which waterfalls do you need to see in Jarabacoa?

Salto de Jimenoa 1 (115 feet, crossed by a hanging bridge), Salto Baiguate (more accessible, great for families) and Salto de Jimenoa 2 (the most spectacular, requires more hiking).

What can you do at Rancho Baiguate?

Rafting on the Yaque del Norte river, canyoning, ziplines, and camping. It's the go-to spot for adventure activities in Jarabacoa, with rafting from $45–65 per person.

How much does a trip to Jarabacoa cost?

A 4-night trip for two people can run $1,200–1,900 total, including flight, rental car, cabin, excursions, and food — notably cheaper than Punta Cana.

Do you need a car to get around Jarabacoa?

Yes, it's practically required. There's no public transportation between the attractions (waterfalls, Rancho Baiguate, strawberry farms), so renting a car from SDQ is recommended.
