Travel Insurance for the Dominican Republic: Is It Worth It?

It's not required, but it's one of those things you're glad to have when something goes wrong — a cancellation, a lost bag, or a medical emergency in a country where your US insurance probably doesn't cover you.

Why Consider It Even Though It's Not Required

Most US health insurance, including Medicare, doesn't cover you outside the country. If something happens in the DR — from food poisoning to a sprained ankle at the beach — you end up paying out of pocket, and private clinics for tourists aren't cheap.

Travel insurance is different: it's built exactly for those unexpected moments on a trip abroad, and during rainy season or if you're traveling with kids or older family members, the small cost is worth the peace of mind.

What It Usually Covers

Medical emergencies and hospitalization, trip cancellation or interruption, lost or delayed luggage, and on some plans even medical evacuation. The exact details vary by plan, so always read the fine print before buying.

Buy it as soon as you book: if you get the insurance the same day you buy your flight or hotel, you're also covered if something forces you to cancel before the trip — not just during it.

How Much It Costs

It depends on how many days you'll be there, your age, and the type of coverage you choose, but in most cases it's a small expense compared to what a medical emergency paid out of pocket abroad would cost.
